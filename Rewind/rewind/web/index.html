Why a deleted photo can still come back

A photo is just bytes stored somewhere. "Delete" almost never destroys those bytes straight away. It removes the pointer to them, or moves them to a bin, or leaves other copies untouched. Rewind looks everywhere a copy or a trace can survive, then compares each one with your current library. Anything that survives somewhere but is no longer in your library is shown as deleted.

  1. Bins: Photos' Recently Deleted, Android's hidden .trashed- files, gallery bins, Drive/OneDrive Trash, computer Trash.
  2. Other copies: old phone backups, chat exports, email attachments, Takeout exports, copies on cards and drives, the other person in a chat.
  3. Previews: thumbnail caches on phones and computers, previews inside app databases, which often outlive the photo.
  4. Raw leftovers: on memory cards, USB sticks and older hard drives, the bytes stay in free space until overwritten.

Where nothing can bring a photo back

When the bin's timer has run out on the service's servers; when a phone's internal storage freed the space (phones encrypt every file and wipe freed blocks); when an SSD has trimmed the space; and when no other copy ever existed. Rewind tells you honestly when that is the case, and lists what it knows existed so you can ask the other person in a chat to resend it.

Rewind only reads. It never deletes or changes anything on your phone, card or accounts, except when you press a "Restore in Google Drive / OneDrive" button on an item.
